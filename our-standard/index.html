The standard: what a mover has to show us

This is the checklist behind every match. Use it on any company you are considering, whether or not it came through us.

  1. Gate 1

    Colorado PUC household-goods permit

    Movers working inside Colorado need a permit from the Public Utilities Commission. Its number, written HHG followed by digits, belongs in the mover’s ads along with a street address (4 CCR 723-6-6016). We read the number, then confirm it with the PUC. See the advertising rule.

  2. Gate 2

    Federal registration for state-line moves

    For moves out of or into Colorado, we ask for the USDOT number and look at FMCSA records for active household-goods authority, via Protect Your Move and the SAFER Company Snapshot.

  3. Gate 3

    Insurance in force

    We ask for proof of current coverage and compare it with the minimums on the permits and insurance page.

  4. Gate 4

    BBB A+ with complaints answered

    We read the company’s BBB profile for the grade and for how complaints were handled. More on the BBB grades page.

  5. Gate 5

    Paperwork that matches

    The name on the permit, the website, the estimate and the truck should be the same. If the names differ we ask why, and we may decline.

What this standard cannot promise

It cannot tell you how a crew will treat your piano on a hot Saturday. Passing the gates means a company is legal, covered and not visibly ignoring customers. After that you still need a written estimate, an in-person or video survey, and a read of the contract. Our phone script covers that.

Why we publish the method instead of names

A name list invites arguments about who paid to be on it. A method can be checked by you in about ten minutes, and it works on movers we will never work with.
