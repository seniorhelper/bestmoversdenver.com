What a BBB grade actually measures

We require an A+. Here is why, and why we do not stop there.

The seven inputs

According to BBB’s own overview of ratings, a grade is built from points added or deducted across:

  1. Complaint history: how many, how they were answered, whether problems repeat.
  2. Type of business, which can lower a grade where the field raises marketplace concern.
  3. Time in business.
  4. Transparency: missing ownership or service information, an address that cannot be verified.
  5. Failure to honor commitments to BBB, such as a mediation settlement.
  6. Licensing, where competency licensing is required and absent.
  7. Advertising issues, rated major, moderate or minor.

The scale

The score is out of 100. A+ runs from 97 to 100, A from 94, A- from 90, then B+, B, B-, C+, C, C-, D+, D, D- in three-point steps, and F is 0 to 59.99. A business can also show NR, meaning no rating, which BBB says is not negative.

What it leaves out

  • Customer reviews are not used in the grade.
  • BBB states a rating does not guarantee reliability or performance.
  • A strong grade says little about how a particular crew handles stairs, weather or heavy items.

How we use it

An A+ is a gate, because it filters for companies with few unresolved complaints, a verifiable address and no advertising problems. Then we read the complaints themselves. A few resolved ones are normal for a business that handles thousands of jobs; a stack of identical, unanswered ones is not. We do not display any grade on this site, since it can change any day.

Do your own look

Search the company on bbb.org, open the complaints tab, and read the replies. Compare the legal name there with the name on the permit.
