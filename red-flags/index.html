Red flags we treat as automatic stops

Each of these is something you can see before money changes hands. One alone is enough to walk away or at least demand an explanation in writing.

  • No HHG number in the ad

    Colorado rules require the permit number and a physical address in every mover advertisement. Missing either means the company is not following basic rules.

  • A different name on each document

    The advertising rule requires the name on the permit. If the web page says one thing, the estimate another and the truck a third, treat it as unresolved until explained.

  • Estimate by phone only, for a full house

    A guess without anyone seeing your belongings is how totals change later. The PUC recommends an inspection. Ask for an in-person or video survey.

  • A contract with blanks

    Colorado requires itemized rates, dates, addresses, payment options and the permit statement. Do not sign pages that leave these open.

  • Only one way to pay

    Colorado requires at least two accepted payment types to be offered. A company that insists on cash alone is outside what the statute describes.

  • No storage address

    If your things will be held anywhere, the contract has to name the location. A mover who is vague about where the truck will park overnight deserves another question.

  • Pressure to sign today

    A good estimate is still good tomorrow. Urgency is a sales tactic.

  • Complaints with no replies

    On the BBB page, replies show whether anyone is listening.

The first six rest on the Colorado rules and statute linked on the permits and insurance page, and on PUC advice. The last two are our own screening judgment.
