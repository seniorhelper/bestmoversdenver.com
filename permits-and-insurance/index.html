Permits and insurance: the numbers behind the gates

Two different authorities cover Denver movers, depending on whether the truck crosses a state line.

What a Colorado contract must say

C.R.S. 40-10.1-505 requires the written contract to state that the company is permitted with the Colorado PUC as a mover, along with the permit number, the mover’s contact details, itemized rates, storage location where relevant, and at least two accepted payment types. Source: C.R.S. 40-10.1-505.

Insurance minimums, with a caveat

The Commission’s financial-responsibility rule is 4 CCR 723-6-6008 (index of the rules). A compliance publisher’s summary of the requirements reads:

  • $750,000 combined single limit for vehicles of 10,000 pounds GVWR or more.
  • $300,000 combined single limit for vehicles under 10,000 pounds.
  • $500,000 general liability for household goods movers.
  • Cargo liability of $10,000, or 60 cents per pound per article, whichever is greater.

Caveat: these figures come from a J.J. Keller summary, not from the rule text. The PUC’s own site blocked our automated reader when we prepared this page, so we have not independently confirmed them. Ask the PUC for the current figures before you rely on any number here.

What insurance does and does not do for you

A mover’s liability policy protects against certain accidents and damage. The cargo limit is what the mover may owe for your goods, and a per-pound figure is far below replacement cost for electronics or art. Ask what valuation options the company offers and what they cost, and consider separate coverage for valuables.

Interstate rights worth knowing

FMCSA’s booklet Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move (49 CFR Part 375, Appendix A) says a mover must provide a written estimate, may charge for a binding estimate but not a non-binding one, and may not amend an estimate after loading. See the text.
